## Chapter 4 Examples

### Continuous Distributions

- $X, Y$ have joint pdf of $f(x,y)$. Then for a function $\mu(X,Y):$
	- $\mathbb{E}(\mu(X,Y)) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \int_{-\infty}^\infty \mu(x,y)f(x,y)dxdy$
- Recall: $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}\left[ (X-\mu_{X})(Y-\mu_{Y}) \right]$ where $\mu_{X} = \mathbb{E}(X);\ \mu_{Y} = \mathbb{E}(Y)$
	- another formula for covariance
		- $\operatorname{Cov}(x,y) = \mathbb{E}(XY) - \mu_{X}\mu_{Y}$
	- Correlation:
		- $\rho_{X,Y} = \dfrac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sqrt{ \operatorname{Var}( X ) }\sqrt{ \operatorname{Var}( Y ) }}$
			- $= \dfrac{Cov(X,Y)}{\sigma_{X}\sigma_{Y}} = \dfrac{\sigma_{X,Y}}{\sigma_{X}\cdot \sigma_{Y}}$
			- where $\sigma_{X} = SD_{X}$ and $\sigma_{Y} = SD_{Y}$ and $\sigma_{X,Y} = Cov(X,Y)$

#### Example

- $X$ and $Y$ are random variables such that $f(x,y) = cxy;\ 0 < y < x < 1$ and 0 elsewhere

1. Find $c$ so that $f(x,y)$ is a joint pdf.
	- begin by plotting the constraints to see what we're dealing with 
		- and identify which area we integrate over
	- $\Rightarrow \int_{0}^1 \int_{0}^x cxy\ dy\,dx = 1$
		- $=\int_{0}^1 cx \left( \frac{y^2}{2}\bigg|_{0}^x \right)dx = 1$
		- $= c \int_{0}^1 \frac{x}{2} (x^2-0)dx = 1$
		- $= \frac{c}{2} \int_{0}^1 x^3 dx = 1$
		- $= \frac{c}{2} \frac{x^4}{4}\bigg|_{0}^1 = 1$
		- $\frac{c}{8} (1^4 - 0^4) = 1$
		- $= \frac{c}{8} = 1$
		- $c= 8$
	- Joint pdf is
		- $\boxed{ f(x,y) = \begin{cases} 8xy & 0 < y < x < 1 \\ 0 & \mathrm{elsewhere} \end{cases} }$

2. Find the marginal pdf of $X$ and the marginal pdf of $Y$.
	- ask yourself what is the range of $X$
		- range/support of $X$ is $S_{X} = (0,1).$
		- support of $Y$ is $S_{Y} = (0,1)$
	- $f_{X}(x) = \int_{y} 8xy\ dy \quad x \in S_{X} = (0,1)$
		- $y$ should vary between $0$ and $x$
		- $\Rightarrow \int_{0}^x 8xy\ dy = 8x \int_{0}^x y dy = 8x \frac{y^2}{2}\bigg|_{0}^x = 4x(x^2-0) = 4x^3$
		- The marginal pdf of $X$ is $\boxed{ f_{X}(x) = \begin{cases} 4x^3 & 0<x<1 \\ 0 & \mathrm{elsewhere} \end{cases} }$
	- Now find the marginals of $Y$ 
		- $f_{Y}(y)  =\int 8xy\ dx$
			- need to integrate over all possible values of x over y and 1
			- $\Rightarrow \int_{y}^1 8xy \ dx = 8y \int_{y}^1 dx = 8y \cdot \frac{x^2}{2} \bigg|_{y}^1 = 4y(1-y^2)   = 4y - 4y^3$
		- Thus, the marginal pdf of Y:
			- $\boxed{ f_{Y} = \begin{cases} 4y-4y^3 & 0 < y < 1 \\ 0 & \mathrm{elsewhere} \end{cases} }$

3. Find $\mu_{x}, \mu_{Y}, \ \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y);\ \sigma_{\scriptsize X}^2, \sigma_{\scriptsize Y}^2,\ \rho_{\scriptsize X,Y}$
	- Find expectation of $X$ $$\mu_{X} = \mathbb{E}(X) = \int_{0}^1 x \cdot 4x^3 dx = 4 \int_{0}^1 x^4 dx = 4 \frac{x^5}{5} \Bigg|_{0}^1 = \frac{4}{5}(1-0) = \frac{4}{5}$$
	- Find expectation of $Y$ 
		- $\mu_{Y} = \mathbb{E}(Y) = \int_{0}^1 y (4y-4y^3)dy = 4 \int_{0}^1 y^2 - y^4 \ dy = 4 \left( \dfrac{y^3}{3} - \dfrac{y^5}{5} \right)\bigg|_{0}^1$
			- $= 4\left( \dfrac{1}{3} - \dfrac{1}{5} \right) = 4 \cdot \dfrac{(5-3)}{15}$
			- $\boxed{ \mu_{Y} = \dfrac{8}{15} }$
	- Find Variance of $X$
		- $\operatorname{Var}( X ) = \mathbb{E}(X^2) - \underbrace{ \left( \mathbb{E}(X) \right)^2 }_{ \mu_{\scriptsize X}^2 }$
		- $\mathbb{E}(X^2) = \int_{0}^1 x^2 \cdot 4x^3 dx = 4 \int_{0}^1 x^5 \ dx = 4 \cdot \dfrac{x^6}{6}\bigg|_{0}^1 = \dfrac{4}{6}(1-0) = \dfrac{4}{6}$
		- $\operatorname{Var}( X ) = \frac{4}{6} - \left( \frac{4}{5} \right)^2 = \frac{4}{6} - \frac{16}{25} = \dfrac{4(25)-6(16)}{6(25)} = \dfrac{100-96}{150} = \dfrac{4}{150} = \dfrac{2}{75}$
		- $\sigma_{\scriptsize X}^2 = \dfrac{2}{75} \implies \boxed{ \sigma_{\scriptsize X} = \sqrt{ \frac{2}{75} } }$
	- Find Variance of $Y$
		- $\operatorname{Var}( Y ) = \mathbb{E}(Y^2) - \left( \mathbb{E}(Y) \right)^2$
		- $\mathbb{E}(Y^2) = \int_{0}^1 y^2 (4y-4y^3)dy = 4 \int_{0}^1 y^3 - y^5 dy = 4 \left( \dfrac{y^4}{4} - \dfrac{y^6}{6}\bigg|_{0}^1 \right) = 4 \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{6} \right) = \dfrac{4(6-4)}{24} = \dfrac{4(2)}{8 \cdot 3} = \dfrac{1}{3}$
		- $\operatorname{Var}( Y ) = \dfrac{1}{3} - \left( \dfrac{8}{15} \right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{3} - \dfrac{64}{225} = \dfrac{11}{225}$
		- $\sigma_{\scriptsize Y}^2 = \dfrac{11}{225} \implies \boxed{ \sigma_{\scriptsize Y} = \sqrt{ \dfrac{11}{225} } }$
	- Find covariance between $X$ and $Y$
		- $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}(XY) - \mu_{\scriptsize X}\mu_{\scriptsize Y}$
		- $\mathbb{E}(XY) = \int_{0}^1\int_{0}^x xy (8xy)\ dy\,dx = \int_{0}^1 8x^2 \int_{0}^x y^2 \ dy\,dx = \int_{0}^1 (8x^2 \cdot \dfrac{y^3}{3}\bigg|_{0}^x)dx = \dfrac{8}{3} \int_{0}^1 x^2 \cdot (x^3 - 0)dx = \dfrac{8}{3} \int_{0}^1 x^5 dx = \dfrac{8}{3} \cdot \dfrac{x^6}{6}\bigg|_{0}^1 = \dfrac{4}{9} (1-0) = \dfrac{4}{9}$
		- $\Rightarrow \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \dfrac{4}{9} - \dfrac{4}{5} \cdot \dfrac{8}{15} = \boxed{ \dfrac{4}{225} }$
			- need to divide to see the strength of the correlation, but as long as the covariance is positive, then they're moving in the same direction
	- Find correlation
		- The correlation coefficient between $X$ and $Y$ is $\rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} = \dfrac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_{\scriptsize X}\sigma_{\scriptsize Y}} = \dfrac{4 / 225}{\sqrt{ (2\times 3) / (75\times3) }\sqrt{ 11 /225 }} = \dfrac{4 / 225}{\frac{\sqrt{ 6 }}{\sqrt{ 225 }} \frac{\sqrt{ 11 }}{\sqrt{ 225 }}} = \dfrac{4 / 225}{\sqrt{ 66 }/225} = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{ 66 }} = 0.492 = \boxed{ 49.2\% }$
			- recall that $-1 \leq \rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} \leq 1$ or else you did something wrong
			- if you get 100\% then that means one is a linear combination of another $Y=aX+b$
