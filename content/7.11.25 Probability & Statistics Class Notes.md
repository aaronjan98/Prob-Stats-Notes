## Multivariate RVS
### Expectations

- Suppose $X, Y$ have joint pmf $\mathbb{P}(x,y).$
	- Let $\mu(X,Y)$ be a function of $X, Y.$
	- Then:
		- $\mathbb{E}(\mu(X,Y)) = \sum_{x,y} \mu(x,y) \mathbb{P}(x,y)$
			- $x,y$ in $\sum_{x,y}$ means a double sum $\sum_{y \in D_{Y}}\sum_{x \in D_{X}} \mu(x,y)\mathbb{P}(x,y)$
		1. Set $\mu(X, Y) = X$
			- $\Rightarrow \mathbb{E}(X) = \sum_{x,y} x P(x,y)$
		2. Set $\mu(X,Y) = Y$
			- $\mathbb{E}(Y) = \sum_{x,y} yP(x,y)$
		- **N.B.**: can use the univariate technique with marginals or use joint for expectation

- **Example.** A survey in a suburban area gives the joint distribution of the number $X$ TVs and the number $Y$ of cars in each household for that area in the table below. $$
	\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|}
	\hline
	x \text{\\} y & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
	\hline
	1 & 0.2 & 0 & 0.1 \\
	2 &  0.1 & 0.2  & 0  \\
	3 &  0.1 &  0.1 & 0.1 \\
	4 & 0  & 0.1 &  0 \\
	\hline
	\end{array}
	$$
	1. Find $\mathbb{E}(X), \mathbb{E}(Y),$ and $\mathbb{E}(X+Y)$
- **Answers.**
	- Recall: $\mathbb{E}(X) = \sum_{x,y} x\mathbb{P}(x,y)$
		- $= 1 \cdot \mathbb{P}(1,1) + 1 \cdot \mathbb{P}(1,2) + 1 \cdot \mathbb{P}(1,3) + 2 \mathbb{P}(2,1) + 2\mathbb{P}(2,2) + 2\mathbb{P}(2,3) + 3\mathbb{P}(3,1) + 3 \mathbb{P}(3,2) + 3\mathbb{P}(3,3) + 4\mathbb{P}(4,1) + 4\mathbb{P}(4,2) + 4\mathbb{P}(4,3)$
		- $= 1 (0.3) + 2(0.3) + 3(0.3) + 4(0.1) = 1.8 + 0.4 = \boxed{ 2.2 }$
			- sum each rows and factor out $X$ 
			- what it means is that on average there are 2.2 TVs per household
	- Now compute $\mathbb{E}(Y)$
		- $\mathbb{E}(Y) = 1 (0.2 + 0.1 + 0.1) + 2 (0.2 + 0.1 + 0.1) + 3(0.1 + 0.1)$
			- $= 0.4 + 2(0.4) + 3(0.2) = 0.4 + 0.8 + 0.6 = \boxed{ 1.8 }$
			- this is the mean of the amount of cars per household
	- $\mathbb{E}(X + Y) = \sum (x+y)P(x,y)$
		- $=\mathbb{E}(X) + \mathbb{E}(Y) = 2.2 + 1.8 = 4$
			- **N.B.**: expectation is a linear operator
			- what it means: on average, we have 4 of those items per household regardless of which items it is

### Covariance and Correlation between Two random variables $X$ and $Y$.

- Suppose $X$ and $Y$ are discrete with joint pmf $\mathbb{P}(x,y).$
	- The covariance between $X$ and $Y$ is given by $\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y)$
	- **N.B.**: covariance means how much $X$ and $Y$ varies together (vary up/down/independent)
		- independent covariance is 0.
	- Recall: $\mathbb{E}\left( (X - \mathbb{E}(X))^2 \right) = \mathbb{E}\left( (X - \mathbb{E}(X))(X-\mathbb{E}(X)) \right)$
		- **N.B.**: expectation of variance with itself
	- $\Rightarrow \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}\left( (X-\mathbb{E}(X))(Y-\mathbb{E}(Y) \right)$
		- another formula for covariance: $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}(XY) - \mathbb{E}(X)\mathbb{E}(Y)$
			- **N.B.**: see it as a more general version of variance formula
			- because of the other variance formula $\mathbb{E}(X^2) - \left( \mathbb{E}(X) \right)^2$
		- Rewrite covariance with formula
			- Set $\mu_{X} = \mathbb{E}(X);\ \mu_{Y} = \mathbb{E}(Y)$
			- Thus, $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$ becomes $\mathbb{E}\left[ (X-\mu_{X})(Y-\mu_{Y}) \right]$
			- the formula becomes: $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}(XY) - \mu_{X}\mu_{Y}$
		- Just need to foil to show both equations are the same
			- $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}\left( (X-\mu_{X})(Y-\mu_{Y}) \right)$
				- $=\mathbb{E}\left( XY - X\mu_{Y} - \mu_{X}Y + \mu_{X}\mu_{Y} \right)$
				- $=\mathbb{E}(XY) - \mu_{Y}\mathbb{E}(X) - \mu_{X}\mathbb{E}(Y) + \mu_{X}\mu_{Y}$
				- $= \mathbb{E}(XY) - \mu_{X}\mu_{Y} - \mu_{X}\mu_{Y} + \mu_{X}\mu_{Y}$
				- $= \mathbb{E}(XY) - \mu_{X}\mu_{Y}$
				- **N.B.**: same as variance

2. Correlation  Coefficent $\rho$ between $X$ and $Y$
	- $\rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} = \dfrac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sqrt{ \operatorname{Var}( X ) }\sqrt{ \operatorname{Var}( Y ) }}$
		- Set $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \sigma_{X,Y}$
			- $\operatorname{Var}( X ) = \sigma_{\scriptsize X}^2$, then
			- $\operatorname{Var}( X ) = \sigma^{2}_{\scriptsize Y}$
		- $\rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} = \dfrac{\sigma_{\scriptsize X,Y}}{\sigma_{\scriptsize X} \cdot \sigma_{\scriptsize Y}}$
	
- Fact: $-1 \leq \rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} \leq 1$ i.e. $\lvert \rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} \rvert \leq 1$
	- because it's a correlation, has to between -1 and 1
	- **N.B.:** Not going to prove it, but definitely need to remember
	- covariance is what drives the sign (denominator is always positive)
		- tells you how much correlation between two random variables
	
	- $\rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} = 1$ or $-1$ if and only if $Y = aX+b$ for some constant $a, b$

#### Properties of Covariance

1. $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \operatorname{Cov}(Y,X)$
	- **N.B.**: this is obvious because flipping the table doesn't change the results
		- also rearrange the terms in the expectation formula doesn't change result
2. $\operatorname{Cov}(X,X) = \operatorname{Var}( X ).$
3. $\operatorname{Cov}(X, c) = 0$; where $c$ is a constant
	- Formula for this would be $\mathbb{E}\bigg( (X-\mu_{X})\underbrace{ (c-\mathbb{E}(c) }_{ c-c=0 } \bigg) = \mathbb{E}(0) = 0.$
4. $\operatorname{Cov}(\underbrace{ aX+b }_{ X }, \underbrace{ cY+d }_{ Y })$ where $a, b, c, d$ are constant
	- **N.B.:** Helpful to think of those two terms as big X and big Y
	- foil the parameters
		- $= ac \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) + ad \operatorname{Cov}(X, 1)$
			- formula for this is expectation of big $X$ and big $Y$ 
				- $\mathbb{E}(ax+b) = a\mu_{X}+b$
			- $=\mathbb{E}\left( (aX + b - a \mu_{x}-b)(cY+d-c\mu_{Y}-d) \right)$
			- $=\mathbb{E}\left( (aX - a\mu_{x})(cY-c\mu_{Y}) \right)$
			- $= \mathbb{E}\left( a(X-\mu_{X})c(Y-\mu_{Y}) \right)$
			- $= ac \mathbb{E}\left( (X - \mu_{X})(Y-\mu_{Y}) \right)$
			- $=ac \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$
5. $\operatorname{Var}( aX + bY + c ) = a^2 \operatorname{Var}( X ) + b^2\operatorname{Var}( Y ) + 2ab \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$
	- the variance of $\operatorname{Var}( aX+bY+c )$ is $\operatorname{Cov}(ax + bY + c, aX + bY + c)$ and foil
		- $= a^2 \operatorname{Cov}(X,X) + ab \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) + ab\operatorname{Cov}(Y,X) + b^2\operatorname{Cov}(Y,Y)$
		- $= \boxed{ a^2 \operatorname{Var}( X ) + 2ab \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) + b^2\operatorname{Var}( Y ) }$
		- **N.B.**: need to remember this formula

- **Remark:**
	- When $X$ and $Y$ are independent
		- $\mathbb{E}(XY) = \mathbb{E}(X) \cdot \mathbb{E}(Y)$
			- **N.B.**: just like how we showed independence with $\mathbb{P}(x,y) = \mathbb{P}_{\!\scriptsize X}(x)\mathbb{P}_{\!\scriptsize Y}(y)$
	1. If $X$ and $Y$ are independent
		- what is the covariance between $X$ and $Y$?
			- $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = 0$
			- $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = \mathbb{E}(XY) - \mu_{\scriptsize X}\mu_{\scriptsize Y}$
				- $=\mathbb{E}(X)\mathbb{E}(Y) - \mu_{\scriptsize X}\mu_{\scriptsize Y}$
				- $=\mu_{\scriptsize X}\mu_{\scriptsize Y} - \mu_{\scriptsize X}\mu_{\scriptsize Y} = 0$
				- this means the random variables vary independently (different directions)

- If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then
	- $\rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} = 0$; because $\rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} = \dfrac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_{\scriptsize X}\sigma_{\scriptsize Y}} = \dfrac{0}{\sigma_{\scriptsize X}\sigma_{\scriptsize Y}} = 0.$
- **Remark**: Just because $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = 0$ does not mean that $X$ and $Y$ are independent.
	- even though the covariance of two independent variables is 0, it is not necessarily true the other way around 

- **Example.** From TV's and Car's
- Compute Variance and $\rho$ of this table $$
	\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|}
	\hline
	x \text{\\} y & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
	\hline
	1 & 0.2 & 0 & 0.1 \\
	2 &  0.1 & 0.2  & 0  \\
	3 &  0.1 &  0.1 & 0.1 \\
	4 & 0  & 0.1 &  0 \\
	\hline
	\end{array}
	$$
1. $\operatorname{Cov}(XY) = \mathbb{E}(XY) - \mathbb{E}(X) \cdot \mathbb{E}(Y)$
	- since we already computed $\mathbb{E}(X)$ and $\mathbb{E}(Y)$
	- we start with
	- $\mathbb{E}(XY) = 1.1 \cdot 0.2 + 1.2(0) + 1 \cdot 3 (0.1) + 2(1)(0.1) + 2(2)0.2 + 2(3)(0) + 3(1)0.1 + 3(2)0.1 + 3(3)+0.1 + 4 \cdot 2 (0.1) = 4.1$
		- recall that $\mu(X,Y) = XY$
	- $\mathbb{E}(X^2) = 1^2 (0.3) + 2^2 (0.3) + 3^2(0.3) + 4^2(0.1) = 5.8$
	- $\mathbb{E}(Y^2) = 1^2(0.4) + 2^2(0.4) + 3^2(0.2) = 3.8$
	
	- $\Rightarrow \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y) = 4.1 - 2.2(1.8) = 0.14$
	- $\operatorname{Var}( X ) = 5.8 - 2.2^2 = 0.96$
	- $\operatorname{Var}( Y ) = 3.8 - 1.8^2 = 0.56$
	- $\Rightarrow \rho_{\scriptsize X,Y} = \dfrac{0.14}{\sqrt{ 0.96 }\sqrt{ 0.56 }} = 0.191$
		- mild correlation, not directly related because only about 20 percent correlation between the number of tvs and cars
